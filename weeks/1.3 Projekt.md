## 2. Flowing Movements

### Original Design

The initial design used a branching system in which nine main branches grew from a shared central point. Their movement was controlled by random changes in direction, creating irregular, tree-like structures.

Each branch could split into smaller branches, with up to seven branching levels. The branches retained their assigned colours, and small coloured dots appeared at their endpoints.

### Design Adjustments

I modified the original design to create a more fluid and organic visual experience.

- **Multiple Starting Points:** Instead of growing from a single centre, the structures now emerge from different locations across the canvas.
- **Perlin Noise Field:** Random directional changes were replaced with a continuous noise field, allowing the lines to follow smoother, more natural paths.
- **Flowing Structures:** Multiple fine lines grow alongside one another, forming curved bundles, waves, arcs, and swirling patterns.
- **Reduced Branching:** Branching was reduced to create a softer, more flowing appearance rather than dense, tree-like structures.
- **Colour Gradients:** Instead of assigning one fixed colour to each branch, colours gradually transition along the flowing lines.
- **Particle Effects:** Small particles add texture to the lines, while glowing spheres move with their growing tips.
- **Persistent Trails:** Previously drawn lines remain visible on the black background, gradually building a layered composition.

### Final Result

The design evolved from a centrally organised branching structure into a **generative system of flowing, colourful light trails**.
